🎯 How to interpret each metric
1. LBP (Latency Bound Probability)
"X% of messages arrived under Y ms."

LBP value	                Meaning for your robot
LBP(≤ 10 ms) ≥ 90%	        ✅ Excellent — 9 of 10 commands fast
LBP(≤ 15 ms) ≥ 99%	        ✅ Real-time capable
LBP(≤ 20 ms) ≥ 99.9%	    ✅✅ Industrial-grade
In academic papers, LBP is often stated as:
"99.9% of packets were delivered with latency ≤ 14 ms"
This is the key performance guarantee you cite.

2. 99.9th percentile (worst case)
This is the most important number for safety-critical systems.
It says: "Only 1 in 1000 commands is slower than X ms"
Ignore the Max — it's a rare outlier (e.g., scheduling hiccup, GC pause, network retransmit)
p99.9 is what you report in a datasheet
Industrial target: p99.9 < 2× mean.
Your example: mean = 11.5, p99.9 = 14.3 → ratio = 1.24 ✅ Excellent

3. Jitter (std dev of inter-arrival times)
Two different jitters in the report:
Jitter                  	What it measures	            Target
Std dev of latency	        How much latency itself varies	< 5 ms
Inter-arrival jitter	    How regular the publishing is	< 1 ms
Why it matters: A robot controller with low average but high jitter feels jerky — the arm moves in fits and starts. Low jitter = smooth motion.

🎯 How to use these numbers academically
In a paper or industrial report, write:

"End-to-end control loop latency from PLC command update to robot setpoint publication was measured over 60 seconds (n = 3600 samples). The mean latency was 11.5 ms (σ = 1.3 ms), with the 99.9th percentile at 14.3 ms. Latency Bound Probability analysis showed 100% of samples arrived within 15 ms, demonstrating suitability for soft real-time control of a 6-axis manipulator at 60 Hz."

This is publication-grade wording for a robotics/industrial control paper.
